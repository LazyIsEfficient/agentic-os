# Competitive Teardown Checklist

A teardown is an evidence-anchored profile of a single alternative. It exists to find *defensible* open ground — not to badmouth rivals. Every claim carries a source or is marked `UNVERIFIED`.

## Run one teardown per key alternative

Pick the alternatives that the [landscape map](landscape-mapping.md) put in the direct tier plus the most dangerous indirect/substitute. Five teardowns is usually enough; ten is procrastination.

## The checklist

For each alternative, capture:

1. **Stated positioning.** The slot *they* claim — pull their homepage hero, tagline, and "for X" language verbatim. This is what you must be different from, in the customer's mind.
2. **Target customer.** Who they aim at (segment, size, role). A rival aimed at enterprise leaves SMB open, and vice versa.
3. **Capabilities.** What the product actually does — the features that matter to the buying decision, not the full feature list. Separate table stakes from differentiators.
4. **Pricing & packaging.** Model (seat / usage / flat), entry price, where the price jumps, what's gated. Pricing reveals who they're really built for.
5. **Proof & traction.** Logos, reviews, named customers, category-leader signals. This calibrates how entrenched they are.
6. **Weaknesses.** Where they are structurally weak — not "their UI is dated" (copyable) but "their model only works for teams over 50" (structural). These are your candidate wedges.
7. **Defensibility of their position.** Why do *they* keep their slot? Network effects, switching cost, brand? You need to know what you cannot dislodge.

## Evidence discipline (load-bearing)

- **Every factual claim cites a source**: a URL, a pricing page tier, a doc, a review quote, a dated screenshot. "Competitor X is weak at Y" with no citation is a guess in a confident costume.
- **Mark anything you could not verify** as `UNVERIFIED: <claim>` and stop — do not let it flow into the differentiation decision as if it were fact. A hedge ("probably", "I think") is not a flag; write `UNVERIFIED` literally.
- **Distinguish stated from observed.** "They say they serve enterprise" (their copy) is different from "their pricing starts at $50k/yr" (observed). Both matter; don't conflate them.

## Output

One profile per alternative against the seven-point checklist, with sources inline. The weaknesses and the stated-positioning fields feed directly into the [differentiation decision](differentiation-strategy.md) — the open ground is where their structural weakness meets a customer-relevant buying axis.
