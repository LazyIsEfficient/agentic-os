# Positioning Statement Template

Fill this in. It is the single artifact that captures the chosen position. It is the **input to messaging**, not messaging itself — do not polish the wording for a customer's eyes; capture the strategic decision crisply and hand the words to `marketer`.

## The statement

> **For** `<target customer — a specific "who," not "everyone">`
> **who** `<the need, problem, or job-to-be-done that triggers the purchase>`,
> **our** `<product / category we claim — see category framing>`
> **is a** `<the category frame: the box you want to be put in>`
> **that** `<the single most important benefit / point of difference>`.
> **Unlike** `<the primary competitive alternative — name the real one, incl. "do nothing">`,
> **our product** `<the defensible reason to believe — the difference a rival can't quickly copy>`.

## Worked example (illustrative — replace entirely)

> **For** revenue teams at Series-B+ SaaS companies
> **who** lose deals because reps can't see which content actually moved a buyer,
> **our** product
> **is a** revenue-intelligence platform
> **that** ties every piece of content to pipeline it influenced.
> **Unlike** general CRM reporting or a BI dashboard,
> **our product** learns from the call transcripts and content engagement the CRM never captures.

## Stress-test checklist (every box must pass)

- [ ] **Specific "who."** The target is a nameable segment, not "everyone." If anyone reading thinks "that's me," it's too broad.
- [ ] **Real alternative named.** The "unlike" names the alternative customers actually weigh — including a substitute or "do nothing" — not a strawman.
- [ ] **A rival would object.** If a competitor could put their own name on the "that..." line without flinching, it is not differentiated. Rewrite.
- [ ] **Defensible, not just true.** The "reason to believe" traces to a structural moat (network effect, switching cost, data, unwanted wedge, category ownership) — not a feature shippable in a quarter.
- [ ] **Category frame is deliberate.** The "is a" box determines who you're compared to. Confirm it's the battlefield you want — reframe if the incumbent owns the default category.
- [ ] **Concessions are explicit.** Somewhere alongside this statement, you've written what you are NOT and what you hand to competitors. A position with no concession is a wishlist.
- [ ] **Demand is real.** A customer in the target segment would say "yes, I want that" — the open quadrant isn't empty because nobody wants it.

## Handoff

Once every box passes, this statement plus the conceded-ground note is the brief input for `marketer` / `marketing-shaper`. They turn it into taglines, copy, and campaigns. This skill stops here.
