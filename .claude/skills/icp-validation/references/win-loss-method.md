# Win-Loss Method — why deals close or don't, from the buyer

Win-loss analysis answers the question sales theater cannot: **why did this account actually buy, not buy, or leave?** The rep's CRM note is a hypothesis shaped by quota and ego. The buyer's account is the evidence. Interview the buyer.

## Cohorts to interview

Run all three. Wins alone are survivorship bias; the segment is only validated when the losses and churns line up with the same fit criteria.

- **Won** — recent closed-won, ideally 30–90 days post-close (long enough to be honest, short enough to remember).
- **Lost** — closed-lost where you competed seriously. Skip deals that died from no-decision noise.
- **Churned** — bought, then left. The most expensive signal: they *fit on paper* and still failed. Often the sharpest ICP correction.

Aim for 5–8 interviews per cohort before synthesizing; patterns stabilize fast.

## Interview structure (buyer-side)

Use [../assets/win-loss-interview-guide.md](../assets/win-loss-interview-guide.md) as the fillable script. The arc:

1. **The trigger.** What was happening when you started looking? What changed? (Surfaces the behavioral-fit trigger event.)
2. **The status quo.** What were you doing before? What did "do nothing" cost you? (Tests whether displacement was real or imagined.)
3. **The decision criteria.** What did you actually evaluate on — and in what order? (The *stated* criteria; rank them.)
4. **The real driver.** Walk me through the moment you decided. Who else was in the room? (The *revealed* criterion — often differs from #3.)
5. **The alternatives.** Who else did you consider, including building it yourself or doing nothing? Why them, why not?
6. **(Lost/churned) The dealbreaker.** What would have had to be true for us to win / for you to stay? Separate **couldn't afford** from **wouldn't trust/didn't fit**.

## Separating signal from rep narrative

- The rep says "we lost on price"; the buyer says "the price was fine, we didn't trust the migration." Price was the *script*, trust was the *driver*. Always reconcile the two accounts; the gap is the finding.
- A "won on relationship" is not a repeatable ICP criterion. Discard reasons that don't generalize to a firmographic or behavioral attribute.
- Weight **revealed** drivers (what they did) over **stated** ones (what they say they value).

## Synthesizing into ICP changes

For each interview, extract: the trigger event, the firmographic attributes, the decision driver, and the outcome. Then look across cohorts:

- **Attributes present in wins and absent in losses** → candidate *fit* criteria.
- **Attributes present in losses/churns that you currently call "fit"** → candidate *disqualifiers*.
- **A driver that recurs in churns** → a fit criterion you're missing (they looked right and weren't).

The output is not a report. It is a revised ICP statement plus a disqualification list, fed into [segment-validation.md](segment-validation.md).
