# ICP vs Persona — account fit, not individual archetype

## The distinction

| | ICP (this skill) | Persona (ux-research) |
|---|---|---|
| Unit | The **account** / company / household | The **individual** inside it |
| Describes | Fit: who is worth selling to and why | Archetype: a person's goals, context, frustrations |
| Built from | Won/lost/churned deal evidence | Interviews and observation of users |
| Output | Targeting + disqualification criteria | Empathy + design input |
| Owner | ICP validation (upstream) | ux-research (downstream) |

An ICP says *which companies to pursue and which to reject*. A persona says *what a person in a pursued company needs and how they experience the product*. You need both, in that order: validate the ICP, then build personas inside it. Building personas first means describing the imagined users of a segment you haven't proven buys.

## What goes in an ICP

**Firmographic fit** — the structural attributes of the account:

- Company size (employees, revenue) and stage.
- Industry / vertical and business model (e.g. usage-based SaaS, marketplace, services).
- Geography, regulatory regime, language.
- Tech stack / current tooling (the "do they have the substrate our product needs" test).
- Buying structure (who signs, budget owner, procurement friction).

**Behavioral fit** — what the account *does* that creates and qualifies need:

- The **trigger event** that creates the need (a hire, a funding round, a migration, a compliance deadline, hitting a scale wall).
- How they buy (self-serve vs sales-led, pilot-driven vs top-down).
- What they do today (the status quo you displace; "nothing" is a different ICP from "a competitor").
- Observable intent signals (job postings, tech adoption, growth rate).

If a criterion describes a *person's* role, mood, or individual goal — "a frustrated ops lead who hates spreadsheets" — it is persona material. Hand it to ux-research. The ICP equivalent is the *account-level* version: "companies whose ops run on spreadsheets at >50 employees."

## Why conflating them fails

- A persona-as-ICP can't disqualify an account, so sales chases everyone.
- An ICP-as-persona produces messaging that targets a job title instead of a buying situation, so marketing speaks to people who can't buy.
- The handoff breaks: ux-research can't build a credible persona for a segment whose account-level fit was never validated.

## The one-line test

If you can answer **"should we pursue this inbound company, yes or no, from public firmographic + behavioral signals alone?"** — you have an ICP. If you can only answer it after talking to a person about their feelings, you have a persona.
