# Segment Validation — validate, sharpen, or invalidate

A segment is a hypothesis. This is how evidence promotes it to a validated ICP, sharpens it, or kills it.

## The validation test

A segment is **validated** when all three hold:

1. **Wins cluster on shared fit criteria.** The won accounts share firmographic + behavioral attributes that the broader market does not.
2. **Losses and churns explain themselves by the same criteria.** The deals you lost or churned are predictable from the *absence* of fit (or the presence of a disqualifier) — not random.
3. **The criteria predict forward.** Apply them blind to a holdout set of recent deals; fit should correlate with outcome better than chance.

If wins cluster but losses are random, you have a *correlation*, not an ICP — keep interviewing. If you cannot state criteria that would have predicted the losses, the segment is not yet real.

## Signs your ICP is too broad

Read these as a panel, not individually — any one can have another cause; together they indict the profile.

- **No inbound lead gets disqualified.** If sales pursues essentially everyone, the ICP isn't filtering — it's decoration.
- **Sales cycles are long with high variance.** A real ICP buys for consistent reasons on a consistent rhythm. Wild variance means you're selling to several different segments wearing one label.
- **Close rate doesn't move with effort.** More demos, same conversion → you're adding volume, not fit. Effort can't fix a targeting problem.
- **The team can't say the ICP the same way.** Ask three people to state the ICP; if you get three answers, it isn't validated — it's vibes.
- **CS and Sales disagree on what "success" means** for the segment, because they're serving structurally different accounts.
- **Win reasons don't generalize.** Every win is "great relationship" or "lucky timing" — no repeatable firmographic/behavioral driver.

## From findings to a sharper ICP

1. **Tighten fit criteria.** Replace aspirational attributes ("growth-minded companies") with the discriminating ones the evidence revealed ("companies that migrated billing systems in the last 12 months").
2. **Write explicit disqualifiers.** For every recurring loss/churn pattern, add a "do not pursue" rule: the firmographic or behavioral signal that predicts failure. Disqualifiers are the most valuable output — they save the most wasted effort.
3. **Name the trigger event.** State the behavioral trigger that should gate outbound and qualify inbound, not just the static firmographics.
4. **Narrow before you broaden.** If the evidence supports a tighter segment, take it. Expand only once the narrow segment is proven *and* saturated; expansion before proof re-broadens the ICP you just sharpened.

## Invalidating a segment

Killing a segment is a result, not a failure. Invalidate when: wins don't cluster, the segment can't be disqualified against, or the "wins" are all unrepeatable. Record *why* it failed — the disqualifier that emerges is reusable evidence, and re-testing a dead segment later starts from that record instead of from zero.

## Handoff

The validated ICP + disqualification list flows to ux-research (personas inside the ICP), marketing-shaper (messaging), growth-engine (experiments), and technical-product-management (prioritization). Re-run win-loss each cycle; the ICP is living, not frozen.
