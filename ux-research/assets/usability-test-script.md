## Section 1: Intro (5 minutes)

> Read this nearly word-for-word. The framing matters.

"Thanks so much for joining today. Before we start, I want to set a few things up so we get the most out of our time."

"**First, this is a test of the design, not of you.** There are no right or wrong answers. If something is confusing, that's a problem with the design, not with you — and that's exactly what we're trying to learn."

"**Second, please think out loud as you go.** Tell me what you're looking at, what you're thinking, what you expect to happen, what's confusing. The more I hear from you, the more useful this is."

"**Third, I'm not the designer of what you're about to see.** You can be completely honest. You won't hurt my feelings. Telling me it's confusing helps me; telling me it's great when it's actually confusing doesn't help anyone."

"**Fourth, I can't help you during the tasks.** I'll seem like I'm being unhelpful. That's because if I help you, I won't see what's actually hard. Just do whatever you'd do if I weren't here. If you get really stuck, that's useful information — just tell me what you'd do in real life."

"Any questions before we start? I'm going to begin recording now — is that okay?"

> [Begin recording. Confirm consent on the recording.]

"Just to confirm on the recording: you're okay with us recording this session, using it for research, and you can ask me to stop at any time?"

## Section 3: Tasks (30–40 minutes)

> Read each task as written. Then stop talking. Watch.

### Task 1

**Read aloud:** "_____"

**While they work:**
- Watch where they look, click, hesitate.
- Encourage think-aloud if they go quiet: "What are you thinking?" "What are you looking for?"
- DO NOT help.
- Note: time to first action, time to completion (or abandon), critical incidents, quotes.

**After they finish (or abandon):**
- "What was going through your head as you did that?"
- "Was anything confusing or unexpected?"
- "If you couldn't have done that, what would you have done in real life?"

## Section 4: Debrief (5 minutes)

> Open-ended wrap-up.

- "Thinking back on the whole session, what stood out to you?"
- "If you could change one thing about what you saw, what would it be?"
- "Was there anything you expected to be there that wasn't?"
- "Is there anything you want to share that we didn't talk about?"
- "Thank you so much for your time. We'll send the `<compensation>` within `<timeframe>`. My email is `<email>` if you have questions or want to withdraw your data."

> [Stop recording.]

## Reminders for the moderator

- Don't help. Watch.
- Don't defend the design. Listen.
- Don't read tasks like riddles — read them naturally.
- Don't say "good job!" — it implies there's a wrong answer.
- After each task, ask the post-task questions even if you think you know the answer.
- After a task they failed, normalize: "Lots of people get stuck there. Thanks for sticking with it."
- Take notes lightly; the recording is the source of truth.
- Schedule 30 minutes between sessions for break and notes.
- Pilot first. Always.

## Common moderation traps

- ❌ "Did you mean to click there?" (leading)
- ❌ "Most users would expect..." (telling them what to think)
- ❌ "Just click the button at the top" (helping)
- ❌ "What you didn't see is that this also does..." (selling)
- ❌ "Wasn't that easy?" (leading, closed)
